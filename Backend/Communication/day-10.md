# Day 10: Difficult Conversations & Conflict Resolution

**Week 2 · Interpersonal & Workplace**

## 🎯 Learning Objectives
- Separate the person from the problem in a conflict
- Structure a difficult conversation so it doesn't derail
- De-escalate when a conversation gets heated

## 📚 Topics
- The three conversations inside every difficult conversation (what happened, feelings, identity — per *Difficult Conversations*)
- Separating positions from underlying interests
- De-escalation techniques in the moment
- When to pause a conversation vs push through

## 💡 Core Ideas
- Every difficult conversation is actually three conversations at once: what happened (facts, often disputed), the feelings involved, and what it says about each person's identity — most people only address the first and wonder why it doesn't land.
- In negotiation and conflict, a 'position' (what someone is demanding) is often a proxy for a deeper 'interest' (what they actually need) — addressing the interest resolves conflicts that arguing positions never will.
- If a conversation is escalating and neither person is able to listen, continuing to push almost always makes it worse — naming the escalation and proposing a short pause is a legitimate, skillful move, not a retreat.

## 🛠️ Practice Exercises
- [ ] Think of a current unresolved conflict. Write out separately: the facts (what happened), the feelings involved (yours and theirs), and any identity issue at stake.
- [ ] For that same conflict, identify the underlying INTEREST beneath each side's stated position.
- [ ] Practice a de-escalation phrase ('I want to understand your side, can we slow down for a second?') — use it if you find yourself in any tense exchange this week.

## 🪞 Reflection Prompts
Write these out, don't just think them:

- What's your default pattern in conflict — do you avoid it, escalate it, or something else?
- What underlying interest, not position, is really at stake in the conflict you wrote about today?

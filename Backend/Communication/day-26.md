# Day 26: Remote & Asynchronous Communication

**Week 4 · Advanced & Professional Application**

## 🎯 Learning Objectives
- Choose the right channel and synchronicity for a message
- Write async updates that don't require a meeting to clarify
- Communicate effectively on video calls despite reduced nonverbal signal

## 📚 Topics
- Sync vs async communication trade-offs
- Writing a self-contained async update (context, status, blockers, ask)
- Video call presence: framing, eye contact via camera, muted-mic etiquette
- Overcommunicating on purpose in remote/distributed settings

## 💡 Core Ideas
- Async communication trades speed for clarity — a well-written async update should require zero follow-up meeting to understand, which means it needs MORE upfront structure than a live conversation would, not less.
- On video calls, looking at the camera (not the screen) is what reads as 'eye contact' to the other person — a small, deliberate adjustment that most people never make.
- In remote or distributed teams, communication that would be 'obvious' in person (a raised eyebrow, overheard context) has to be stated explicitly — slightly overcommunicating is usually correct, not excessive.

## 🛠️ Practice Exercises
- [ ] Write an async status update (for a real or hypothetical project) using a Context → Status → Blockers → Ask structure, self-contained enough to need no follow-up meeting.
- [ ] On your next video call, deliberately practice looking at the camera lens (not the screen) when you're speaking to the group.
- [ ] Identify one piece of context you usually assume is 'obvious' to remote teammates, and communicate it explicitly this week.

## 🪞 Reflection Prompts
Write these out, don't just think them:

- What's one meeting this week that could have been an async update instead — and what made it feel like it needed to be live?
- How did it feel, physically, to look at the camera instead of the screen? Did it change how present you felt in the call?

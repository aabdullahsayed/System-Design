# Day 25: Communicating Up: Talking to Leadership

**Week 4 · Advanced & Professional Application**

## 🎯 Learning Objectives
- Structure an update for a senior audience with limited time and context
- Lead with the decision or ask, not the backstory
- Anticipate and preempt likely questions

## 📚 Topics
- The 'executive summary first' principle
- Anticipating questions before they're asked
- Bringing options and a recommendation, not just a problem
- Reading the room's time constraints and adjusting on the fly

## 💡 Core Ideas
- Senior audiences are almost always time-constrained and context-poor for your specific topic — leading with the ask or decision, THEN the supporting detail (only if asked), respects both realities.
- Bringing a problem alone often reads as unprepared; bringing a problem with 2-3 options and a clear recommendation reads as someone who can be trusted with more ownership.
- Anticipating the 2-3 questions a senior stakeholder will likely ask, and having crisp answers ready, is what separates a smooth update from one that spirals into an uncomfortable back-and-forth.

## 🛠️ Practice Exercises
- [ ] Take an update you need to give to someone senior (real or hypothetical) and rewrite it leading with the ask/decision in the first sentence.
- [ ] For a real decision you need input or approval on, prepare 2-3 options with a clear recommendation, not just a description of the problem.
- [ ] Write down the 3 questions you think a senior stakeholder is most likely to ask about that topic, and prepare a one-sentence answer to each.

## 🪞 Reflection Prompts
Write these out, don't just think them:

- What's your instinct when talking to someone senior — do you over-explain out of nervousness, or under-explain out of trying to seem confident?
- What question are you most afraid a senior stakeholder will ask you, and do you actually have a good answer ready?

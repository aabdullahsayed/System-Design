# Day 11: Persuasion & Influence

**Week 2 · Interpersonal & Workplace**

## 🎯 Learning Objectives
- Apply core principles of ethical persuasion
- Build an argument that leads with the audience's interests, not just your own
- Recognize manipulation and avoid it in your own persuasion

## 📚 Topics
- Aristotle's ethos, pathos, logos
- Cialdini's principles of influence (reciprocity, social proof, authority, etc.) at a high level
- Framing your ask around the listener's interests, not just yours
- The line between persuasion and manipulation

## 💡 Core Ideas
- Persuasion that only appeals to logic (logos) and ignores credibility (ethos) and emotional resonance (pathos) is usually weaker than one that uses all three deliberately.
- People are far more persuaded by 'what's in it for them' than by 'why you want it' — reframing your ask around the listener's actual interests is the single biggest lever in everyday persuasion.
- The difference between persuasion and manipulation is transparency and respect for the other person's autonomy — manipulation withholds information or exploits bias; ethical persuasion doesn't need to.

## 🛠️ Practice Exercises
- [ ] Take a request you need to make this week (a raise, a favor, buy-in on an idea) and rewrite your pitch framed entirely around the listener's interests, not yours.
- [ ] Identify one instance of persuasion aimed at you today (an ad, a pitch, a colleague's request) and name which of ethos/pathos/logos it leaned on most.
- [ ] Practice delivering your reframed pitch from exercise 1 to a real person this week, or rehearse it out loud.

## 🪞 Reflection Prompts
Write these out, don't just think them:

- Where's the line, for you, between skillful persuasion and manipulation? Can you think of a case where you or someone else crossed it?
- How did reframing your ask around the listener's interest change how it felt to make it?

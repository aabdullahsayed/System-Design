# Day 1: What Communication Actually Is

**Week 1 · Foundations**

## 🎯 Learning Objectives
- Understand communication as a two-way, receiver-defined process
- Identify your default communication style
- Set a baseline to measure growth against over the next 30 days

## 📚 Topics
- The sender-message-receiver-feedback model
- Verbal, nonverbal, and written communication channels
- Communication styles: passive, aggressive, passive-aggressive, assertive
- Why 'I said it clearly' doesn't mean it was received clearly

## 💡 Core Ideas
- Communication is defined by what the listener understood, not what the speaker intended — 'I said it clearly' is not the same as 'it was received clearly.'
- Every message travels through multiple channels at once (words, tone, body language) — when they conflict, people trust the nonverbal channel first.
- Most people default to one of four styles (passive, aggressive, passive-aggressive, assertive) under stress, often different from how they communicate when calm.

## 🛠️ Practice Exercises
- [ ] Write down one recent conversation that went badly. Identify which part of the sender→receiver→feedback loop broke down.
- [ ] Ask one trusted person to honestly describe your communication style in a few words. Compare it to your own self-perception.
- [ ] Keep a 'communication log' for today: jot down 3 moments where you noticed your tone or body language didn't match your words.

## 🪞 Reflection Prompts
Write these out, don't just think them:

- What's one belief about your own communication ability that today's reading challenged?
- Think of someone you consider a great communicator. What specifically do they do differently from you?

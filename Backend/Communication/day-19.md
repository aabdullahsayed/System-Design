# Day 19: Handling Q&A

**Week 3 · Public Speaking & Presentations**

## 🎯 Learning Objectives
- Answer questions confidently, including ones you don't fully know the answer to
- Buy thinking time gracefully without stalling awkwardly
- Redirect hostile or off-topic questions constructively

## 📚 Topics
- The pause-repeat-answer structure
- Handling 'I don't know' gracefully
- Defusing hostile or loaded questions
- Bridging back to your core message

## 💡 Core Ideas
- Repeating or briefly paraphrasing a question before answering buys you thinking time, ensures the whole room heard it, and confirms you understood it correctly — all in one natural-sounding move.
- 'I don't know, but I'll find out and follow up' is a stronger answer than a rambling guess — confident uncertainty reads better than unconfident false certainty.
- For a hostile or loaded question, acknowledging the underlying concern before responding to the substance ('That's a fair concern about X, here's how I'd think about it...') defuses tension far better than getting defensive.

## 🛠️ Practice Exercises
- [ ] Practice the pause-repeat-answer structure: have someone ask you 3 questions on a topic you know, and repeat/paraphrase each before answering.
- [ ] Prepare and practice saying 'I don't know, but here's how I'd find out' out loud for a question you genuinely can't answer about your field.
- [ ] Write out a hostile or skeptical question someone might realistically ask you, and draft a calm, non-defensive response.

## 🪞 Reflection Prompts
Write these out, don't just think them:

- What's your instinctive reaction to a question that catches you off guard — do you freeze, ramble, or get defensive?
- How did it feel to practice saying 'I don't know' out loud instead of bluffing?

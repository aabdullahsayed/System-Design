# Day 20: Impromptu & Off-the-Cuff Speaking

**Week 3 · Public Speaking & Presentations**

## 🎯 Learning Objectives
- Structure a coherent answer on the spot, without preparation
- Use a simple mental framework to organize impromptu remarks
- Reduce the anxiety of being called on unexpectedly

## 📚 Topics
- The PREP framework (Point, Reason, Example, Point)
- Buying a few seconds of thinking time gracefully
- Why structure matters more under pressure, not less
- Practicing impromptu speaking deliberately

## 💡 Core Ideas
- Under pressure, people default to rambling because they're composing sentence-by-sentence instead of point-by-point — a simple structure like PREP (Point, Reason, Example, restate Point) gives your brain a container to fill instead of a blank page.
- A brief, composed pause ('Good question, let me think about that for a second') before answering reads as thoughtful, not unprepared — silence is far less awkward to the room than it feels to the speaker.
- Impromptu speaking is a trainable skill, not a fixed trait — the only way to get more comfortable with it is deliberately practicing being put on the spot, ideally in low-stakes settings first.

## 🛠️ Practice Exercises
- [ ] Pick 3 random, low-stakes prompts (e.g., 'what's your favorite season and why', 'describe your ideal weekend') and practice a 60-second PREP-structured answer to each, out loud, with no prep time.
- [ ] The next time you're unexpectedly asked for your opinion in a meeting or conversation, consciously use the PREP structure.
- [ ] Ask a friend or colleague to fire 3 surprise questions at you and practice the composed pause before answering each.

## 🪞 Reflection Prompts
Write these out, don't just think them:

- How did having a structure (PREP) change how it felt to speak with zero preparation?
- What's your instinct when put on the spot — do you rush to fill silence, or do you freeze? What would help most?

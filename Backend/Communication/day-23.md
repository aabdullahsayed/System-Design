# Day 23: Meeting Facilitation

**Week 4 · Advanced & Professional Application**

## 🎯 Learning Objectives
- Structure a meeting so it starts, stays, and ends on purpose
- Draw out quieter voices and manage dominant ones
- Close a meeting with clear, owned next steps

## 📚 Topics
- Setting a clear purpose and agenda before the meeting
- Facilitation techniques: round-robin, timeboxing, parking lot
- Managing dominant talkers and drawing out quiet ones
- Ending with explicit owners and next steps

## 💡 Core Ideas
- A meeting without a stated purpose or desired outcome almost always runs long and accomplishes less — 'what decision or output do we need by the end of this?' should be answerable before it starts.
- A simple round-robin ('let's go around, 30 seconds each') is one of the most effective tools for balancing airtime — it structurally prevents the loudest person from dominating without any confrontation needed.
- A meeting that ends without explicit owners and deadlines for next steps effectively didn't produce a decision — it produced a discussion.

## 🛠️ Practice Exercises
- [ ] Before your next meeting (that you're running or can influence), write a one-sentence purpose and desired outcome, and share it in the invite/agenda.
- [ ] In that meeting, use a round-robin or direct question ('X, what's your take?') to draw out at least one quieter voice.
- [ ] End the meeting by explicitly stating next steps and owners out loud, and follow up in writing within the hour.

## 🪞 Reflection Prompts
Write these out, don't just think them:

- How did having an explicit purpose change the shape or length of the meeting?
- Who talks the most in your typical meetings, and is that the right allocation of airtime for the topic?

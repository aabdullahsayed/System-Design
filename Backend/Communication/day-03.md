# Day 3: Clarity & Conciseness

**Week 1 · Foundations**

## 🎯 Learning Objectives
- Structure a message so the main point lands immediately
- Cut filler and hedging language that dilutes your point
- Match message length to the listener's actual need

## 📚 Topics
- Leading with the point (the 'BLUF' — bottom line up front)
- Filler words and hedging language
- The curse of knowledge (assuming shared context you haven't stated)
- Right-sizing a message to its audience

## 💡 Core Ideas
- Burying your main point at the end of a long explanation forces the listener to hold everything in working memory just to find out why it mattered — lead with it instead.
- Hedging language ('I think maybe, sort of...') often comes from a desire to seem humble or avoid pushback, but it usually just erodes clarity and confidence.
- The 'curse of knowledge' means you forget what it's like not to know what you know — you have to deliberately state context that feels obvious to you.

## 🛠️ Practice Exercises
- [ ] Take an email or message you'd normally send and rewrite it with the main point in the first sentence.
- [ ] Record yourself explaining a work topic for 60 seconds. Count your filler words ('um', 'like', 'sort of', 'I think maybe'). Redo it aiming for zero.
- [ ] Explain something you know well to someone unfamiliar with it, and note where you assumed context they didn't have.

## 🪞 Reflection Prompts
Write these out, don't just think them:

- Where in your normal communication do you tend to bury the point — email, meetings, or casual conversation?
- What's one phrase or filler word you noticed yourself overusing today?

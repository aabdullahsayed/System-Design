# Day 13: Written Communication & Email Etiquette

**Week 2 · Interpersonal & Workplace**

## 🎯 Learning Objectives
- Write emails and messages that get read and acted on
- Choose the right channel for the right message
- Avoid common written-communication pitfalls (tone loss, wall-of-text, unclear asks)

## 📚 Topics
- Subject lines that set expectations
- Structuring an email/message for scannability
- Tone loss in text — why written messages read colder than intended
- Choosing the right channel: email vs chat vs call vs in-person

## 💡 Core Ideas
- A subject line should tell the reader what to do or expect ('Decision needed by Fri: venue for offsite') rather than just naming a topic ('Offsite') — it changes whether the email gets opened promptly at all.
- Written text strips out tone of voice and facial expression, so neutral text often reads as colder or more curt than intended — a little extra warmth in wording compensates for the lost channel.
- The right channel depends on the message: nuanced or emotionally loaded topics usually belong in a call or in-person conversation, not an email or chat message, no matter how well-written.

## 🛠️ Practice Exercises
- [ ] Rewrite the subject line of your last 3 sent emails to state the action or expectation clearly.
- [ ] Take one email you're about to send and read it back imagining you have no context — is the ask clear in the first two sentences?
- [ ] Identify one upcoming message that's actually better suited to a call or in-person conversation than text, and switch the channel.

## 🪞 Reflection Prompts
Write these out, don't just think them:

- What's one email or message you sent recently that you now realize was misread in tone? What would you change?
- Do you tend to over-explain or under-explain in writing? What's driving that habit?

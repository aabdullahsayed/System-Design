# Day 22: Negotiation Skills

**Week 4 · Advanced & Professional Application**

## 🎯 Learning Objectives
- Prepare for a negotiation using interests, not just positions
- Identify your BATNA (Best Alternative to a Negotiated Agreement)
- Use anchoring and framing deliberately in a negotiation

## 📚 Topics
- Positions vs interests (building on Day 10)
- BATNA and knowing your walk-away point
- Anchoring: who should make the first offer, and why
- Win-win framing vs win-lose framing

## 💡 Core Ideas
- Knowing your BATNA (your best alternative if this negotiation fails) is the single biggest source of real negotiating power — it tells you exactly when to walk away and stops you from accepting a bad deal out of anxiety.
- Whoever anchors first often shifts the entire range of the negotiation toward their number, even when the other side 'knows' anchoring is happening — deliberate, well-researched anchoring is a real advantage.
- Framing a negotiation as solving a shared problem ('how do we both get what we need here') rather than a zero-sum fight often uncovers trades neither side saw at first (e.g., trading a lower price for faster payment terms).

## 🛠️ Practice Exercises
- [ ] Identify an upcoming negotiation (salary, a deal, even a household decision). Write your BATNA and your walk-away point before anything else.
- [ ] For that same negotiation, list both sides' likely underlying INTERESTS, not just stated positions.
- [ ] Practice an opening anchor statement out loud, framed around a shared problem rather than a demand.

## 🪞 Reflection Prompts
Write these out, don't just think them:

- What's your BATNA in the negotiation you identified — and does actually knowing it change how confident you feel?
- What's one trade-off (not just price) you hadn't considered until you thought about both sides' interests?

# Day 8: Assertive Communication

**Week 2 · Interpersonal & Workplace**

## 🎯 Learning Objectives
- Distinguish assertiveness from both passivity and aggression
- State needs and boundaries clearly without apologizing for them
- Use 'I' statements to express disagreement constructively

## 📚 Topics
- Passive vs aggressive vs assertive communication
- 'I' statements vs 'you' statements
- Setting a boundary without over-explaining or apologizing
- Saying no without guilt

## 💡 Core Ideas
- Assertiveness is not the midpoint between passive and aggressive — it's a different axis entirely: stating your needs clearly WHILE respecting the other person's needs, rather than sacrificing either.
- 'You' statements ('You always interrupt me') trigger defensiveness; 'I' statements ('I find it hard to finish my point when I get interrupted') describe your experience without accusing.
- Over-explaining a 'no' invites negotiation — a clear, brief boundary is usually more respected than a long justification.

## 🛠️ Practice Exercises
- [ ] Rewrite 3 'you' statements you've said or thought recently ('you never...', 'you always...') as 'I' statements.
- [ ] Practice saying no to one small request today without over-explaining or apologizing excessively.
- [ ] Identify one situation this week where you were passive when you needed to be assertive. Write the assertive version of what you wish you'd said.

## 🪞 Reflection Prompts
Write these out, don't just think them:

- What makes it hardest for you personally to be assertive — fear of conflict, fear of seeming rude, or something else?
- How did it feel, in the moment, to say no without over-justifying it?

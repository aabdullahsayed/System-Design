# Day 28: Handling Criticism & Difficult Feedback

**Week 4 · Advanced & Professional Application**

## 🎯 Learning Objectives
- Separate useful signal from unhelpful delivery in harsh feedback
- Respond to criticism without becoming defensive or collapsing
- Ask clarifying questions to turn vague criticism into something actionable

## 📚 Topics
- Separating the message from the delivery
- The 'thank you, tell me more' response
- Turning vague criticism into specific, actionable information
- Self-compassion as a prerequisite for hearing hard feedback well

## 💡 Core Ideas
- Harsh or poorly delivered feedback can still contain a true, useful signal — the skill is separating 'how it was said' (which you can set aside) from 'what was actually true in it' (which you can use).
- Responding to criticism with 'thank you, can you say more about that?' does double duty: it buys you composure AND usually surfaces the specific, actionable version of vague criticism.
- People who are harder on themselves in general tend to react more defensively to external criticism — a baseline of self-compassion isn't 'softness', it's what makes it psychologically safe to actually hear hard feedback.

## 🛠️ Practice Exercises
- [ ] Think of a piece of harsh or poorly delivered feedback you received recently. Separate, in writing, the delivery (tone, timing) from the actual content (is there a true point in there?).
- [ ] The next time you receive any critical feedback, practice responding with 'thank you, can you tell me more about that?' before reacting further.
- [ ] Write one self-compassionate reframe of a mistake you're still being hard on yourself about.

## 🪞 Reflection Prompts
Write these out, don't just think them:

- What's your instinctive reaction to harsh feedback — defend, shut down, over-apologize? Did today's exercise shift that at all?
- Looking back at the feedback you analyzed, what was the true, useful part buried inside the delivery?

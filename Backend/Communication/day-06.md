# Day 6: Giving and Receiving Feedback

**Week 1 · Foundations**

## 🎯 Learning Objectives
- Deliver feedback that's specific, actionable, and kind
- Receive feedback without becoming defensive
- Use a simple structure to keep feedback conversations on track

## 📚 Topics
- The SBI model (Situation-Behavior-Impact)
- Positive vs constructive feedback ratios
- Common defensive reactions to feedback and how to notice them in yourself
- Asking for feedback proactively

## 💡 Core Ideas
- Vague feedback ('be more proactive') is nearly impossible to act on — the SBI model (Situation, Behavior, Impact) forces specificity: 'In yesterday's meeting (situation), when you interrupted twice (behavior), it made it harder for others to finish their points (impact).'
- The instinct to explain or defend yourself the moment you receive feedback usually stops you from actually hearing it — the discipline is to say 'thank you, let me think about that' before responding.
- Feedback given only when something goes wrong trains people to dread it — regularly giving specific positive feedback makes the constructive kind land better too.

## 🛠️ Practice Exercises
- [ ] Give one piece of feedback today using the SBI structure (Situation-Behavior-Impact), written or spoken.
- [ ] The next time someone gives you feedback, practice saying 'thank you, let me sit with that' before responding — even internally.
- [ ] Proactively ask one person: 'What's one thing I could do better?' and just listen, without defending.

## 🪞 Reflection Prompts
Write these out, don't just think them:

- How did it feel to ask for feedback proactively instead of waiting to receive it?
- What's your default defensive reaction to critical feedback (explaining, deflecting, shutting down)? Did you notice it today?

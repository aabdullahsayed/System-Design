# Day 18: Visual Aids & Slides

**Week 3 · Public Speaking & Presentations**

## 🎯 Learning Objectives
- Design slides that support a talk instead of replacing it
- Avoid the most common slide design mistakes (text walls, clutter)
- Use visuals to make data and abstract ideas concrete

## 📚 Topics
- Slides as support, not a script
- One idea per slide
- Data visualization basics for presentations
- The 'slideument' trap (slides trying to be both a deck and a document)

## 💡 Core Ideas
- A slide with a wall of text forces the audience to choose between reading and listening to you — they can't do both, and reading usually wins, which means they stop listening.
- The strongest presentation slides carry ONE idea each, often a single image, chart, or short phrase — if you can't summarize a slide's purpose in one sentence, it's probably trying to do too much.
- A deck is either a presentation aid (sparse, works with you talking) or a document (dense, works standalone) — trying to make one deck serve both purposes usually makes it bad at both.

## 🛠️ Practice Exercises
- [ ] Take an existing slide deck (yours or a public example) and identify 3 slides that violate the 'one idea per slide' rule. Redesign one of them.
- [ ] Build a single slide communicating one data point or idea from a project you're working on, using minimal text and one clear visual.
- [ ] Practice a 2-minute talk using that one slide as support, without reading from it.

## 🪞 Reflection Prompts
Write these out, don't just think them:

- What's the worst presentation slide habit you personally fall into (too much text, too many bullets, no visual)?
- How did presenting WITHOUT reading from the slide change your delivery?

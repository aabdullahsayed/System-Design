# Day 15: Overcoming Public Speaking Anxiety

**Week 3 · Public Speaking & Presentations**

## 🎯 Learning Objectives
- Understand the physiology of speaking anxiety
- Apply practical techniques to manage nerves before and during speaking
- Reframe anxiety as usable energy rather than something to eliminate

## 📚 Topics
- The physiology of stage fright (fight-or-flight)
- Reframing arousal as excitement, not fear
- Preparation as the antidote to anxiety
- Grounding techniques (breath, physical presence)

## 💡 Core Ideas
- Public speaking anxiety and excitement produce nearly identical physiological arousal (racing heart, adrenaline) — research shows that consciously relabeling the feeling as 'excited' rather than 'anxious' measurably improves performance.
- Anxiety thrives on uncertainty; the single most reliable way to reduce it isn't a breathing trick, it's over-preparing the first 60 seconds of your talk so well that you don't need to think about them.
- Slow, deliberate breathing directly counteracts the physiological fight-or-flight response — it's not just a mental trick, it changes your actual heart rate and cortisol in real time.

## 🛠️ Practice Exercises
- [ ] Write and fully memorize the first 60 seconds of a talk on any topic you know well.
- [ ] Practice box breathing (4 counts in, 4 hold, 4 out, 4 hold) for 2 minutes before doing something mildly nerve-inducing today (even a phone call you're dreading).
- [ ] Deliver that 60-second opening out loud to a mirror, your phone camera, or another person, and consciously relabel any nervous feeling as 'excitement' beforehand.

## 🪞 Reflection Prompts
Write these out, don't just think them:

- What specifically are you afraid will happen when you speak in front of others? Is that fear realistic?
- How did relabeling nervous arousal as excitement change how the practice felt?

# 30 Days of Communication Skills

A structured, practice-first plan to become a noticeably better communicator in 30 days — at work, in relationships, and in high-stakes moments like negotiations and public speaking.

This course is built around **real conversations, not theory**. Every day pairs a small amount of reading with practice you do in your actual life that day — there's no substitute for trying a skill out loud with a real person.

## How this is organized

```
communication-30-days/
├── README.md          ← you are here
├── PROGRESS.md         ← printable/checkable tracker for all 30 days
├── day-01.md ... day-30.md
```

Each `day-XX.md` file follows the same template:

- **🎯 Learning Objectives** — what you should be able to do by the end of the day
- **📚 Topics** — concepts to read up on
- **💡 Core Ideas** — the key insights worth internalizing, not just memorizing
- **🛠️ Practice Exercises** — real, in-conversation practice for that day (this is where the growth happens)
- **🪞 Reflection Prompts** — write these out; reflection is what turns an experience into a lesson
- **🔗 Resources** — where to go deeper
- **✅ Daily Checklist** — tick off as you go

## The 30-Day Arc

| Week | Days | Theme |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 1–7 | Foundations — listening, clarity, nonverbal communication, questions, feedback |
| 2 | 8–14 | Interpersonal & Workplace — assertiveness, emotional intelligence, conflict, persuasion, cross-cultural communication, writing |
| 3 | 15–21 | Public Speaking & Presentations — anxiety, storytelling, delivery, slides, Q&A, impromptu speaking |
| 4 | 22–30 | Advanced & Professional Application — negotiation, facilitation, networking, communicating up, remote work, personal brand |

Each week closes with a **capstone day** (7, 14, 21) that puts the week into practice in a real, higher-stakes situation, and **Day 30** turns the whole month into a concrete 90-day growth plan.

## Suggested pace

- **~30–60 minutes/day**, but the real practice happens *inside* your normal conversations, not in a separate study session — most days ask you to apply a skill in a meeting, email, or chat you were already going to have.
- Do the reflection prompts **in writing**. Thinking about a conversation and writing about it produce different, complementary kinds of insight — don't skip the writing.
- If a skill doesn't click in one day, keep using it into the next — this plan is a sequence of habits to layer, not a checklist to rush through.

## Prerequisites

- Willingness to try things in real conversations, including some that feel a little uncomfortable at first
- At least one person (colleague, friend, or family member) willing to give you honest feedback occasionally
- A place to write your daily reflections (a notebook or a simple doc works fine)

## What you'll have by Day 30

- A clear, honest picture of your communication style — both self-assessed and externally validated
- Practiced skills in active listening, assertiveness, feedback, and conflict resolution
- A prepared, rehearsed 3–5 minute talk with real delivered feedback behind it
- Real experience applying negotiation, facilitation, and networking skills, not just theory
- A personal go-to story, developed and practiced at three different lengths
- A `COMMUNICATION_GROWTH_PLAN.md` — a concrete 90-day plan for what to keep practicing and how to keep getting feedback

## Tips for success

1. **Practice in real conversations, not just in your head.** Every exercise is designed to happen in an actual interaction today.
2. **Ask for feedback, even when it's uncomfortable.** Days 7, 14, 21, and 29 all involve getting honest input from another person — that external signal is worth more than any self-assessment.
3. **Write your reflections down.** The act of writing surfaces insight that just thinking doesn't.
4. **Expect some awkwardness.** New communication habits feel unnatural before they feel natural — that discomfort is a normal part of the process, not a sign you're doing it wrong.
5. **Keep the 2-3 skills that matter most.** Day 30 is designed to help you narrow 30 days of practice into a few habits you'll actually sustain — that's the real goal, not perfect recall of all 30 days.
